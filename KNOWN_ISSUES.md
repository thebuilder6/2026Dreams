Correct as far as it goes: the old default claimed `opponentObserved = true` with four empty lists.   - **The design problem, which is the actual item.** An honest-but-empty knowledge does **not** degrade the policy to something safe. It empties the utility matrix, so `evaluateLocalUtilityMatrix`'s argmax falls through to whatever carries a non-zero base — measured in the unit tests, that fallback is `RUSH_CLIMB` for a *defender*. Zero-information is indistinguishable from "no good option", and the tiebreak is "drive to the tower". `evaluateLocalUtilityMatrix` needs an explicit no-information path, plus a test asserting a defender with no picture does not climb. That work is independent of whichever default the overload ends up using.
  - **Now measured independently by the decision cards (2026-09-28), which is the evidence this entry asked for.** `tools/score/run-cards.ps1` evaluates every card three times — Blue clairvoyant, mirrored Red clairvoyant, and observed tier — and flags `TIER DIFFERS`. **6 of 39 cards (15%) pick a different objective under the observed tier**, and **five of the six are `TACTICAL_DEFENDER` / `DEFENSE_BULLY` cards whose clairvoyant answer is a defensive objective** (`LEAD_INTERCEPT`, `DENY_SHOOTING_LANE`) **and whose observed answer is `VACUUM_MIDFIELD` or `STAGE_STANDOFF`.** So the defensive policy does not merely degrade under the honest tier — on those cards it **disappears**, and the robot goes harvesting. Note the card set does *not* reproduce the `RUSH_CLIMB` fallback the unit tests showed; it picks a different wrong answer. Same defect class, different symptom, and the two together are the reason a single no-information path (not a per-objective patch) is the fix. Full table: `docs/SCORE_RIG_RESULTS.md` §5a.
  - **Still ~27 test call sites rely on the 2-arg default** (`evaluatePolicy(world, Archetype…)` in `JevDecisionEngineTest`), so the empty-knowledge path is heavily exercised — just no longer *asserted*. Grep for `evaluatePolicy(\w+, Archetype` before changing the default; each one is a test that silently depends on it.
  - **Who is actually affected in production.** Sim AI bots are fine — `AIRobotInstance` passes `buildMatchKnowledgeForSimBot(...)`, i.e. a real `ClairvoyantKnowledge` with live counts. The real robot on hardware is unchanged (it already took the old silent `return 0`). The change in production behaviour is the **co-pilot in sim**, which now sees 0 zone fuel where it previously read perfect sim data: correct in spirit, but an unmeasured shift in the co-pilot's objective mix that nobody has characterised.
